Central Pool Grain Stocks and Targeted Agri Exports

20 November 2026 · Outward-facing MSME reference · Pehowa / Kurukshetra / Jhajjar context · Sources attributed by tier

1 October 2026 snapshot: rice 37.76 MT, wheat 46.32 MT, total 84.08 MT plus unmilled paddy 26.43 MT.

Stocks sit well above buffer norms (rice multiple times the July buffer of ~13.5 MT; wheat elevated versus April and July norms). Surplus supports open-market sales, ethanol diversion and free-ration offtake under PMGKAY. Kharif paddy procurement is underway and robust in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh; slower in several southern and eastern states.

Bajra (pearl millet) and maize are not large central-pool holdings. Bajra wholesale prices in Haryana recently around ₹2,500–2,700 per quintal. Maize production estimates for 2026-27 have been revised lower to approximately 50 MT on weak monsoon effects in key states.

Sources: Igrain citing FCI (18 November 2026, T2); Datawrapper compilation of FCI data (T1); Agmarknet mid-November (T1); USDA FAS / Hindu BusinessLine on maize (T2).

2. Top Exported Farm Produce to Europe, United States, United Kingdom, Canada, France and Italy

April–September FY 2026-27 figures from Ministry of Commerce / APEDA-linked official statements.

Basmati rice

  • Exports rose 25.4% to USD 1.60 billion.
  • United States: USD 139.8 million (+27.7%).
  • Canada and Italy recorded increases (exact country splits not published in the summary releases).
  • Europe is a noted growing market; recent contracts of approximately 0.5 million tonnes of new-season basmati cited to Europe and Middle East buyers.

Yellow dent corn (maize)

  • Exports rose 1,156% to USD 122.82 million.
  • Primary buyers are in East and Southeast Asia (Vietnam dominant). Not a major flow to the listed Western destinations in the reported data.

Processed / frozen potato

  • Exports rose 20.8% to USD 179.4 million.
  • Demand strengthening in East and Southeast Asia; Europe is a secondary destination for processed items.

Broader context: overall agri exports (April–October) rose to approximately USD 27.88 billion. EU shipments increased in the period (Italy +25%, France +9% in one reporting window); United Kingdom +7%. Top overall agri destinations remain UAE, Saudi Arabia, USA and others. Basmati, marine products, spices, buffalo meat, processed foods and millets form the core basket.

Sources: Ministry of Commerce / APEDA statements via IANS, DD India, AgriInsite, Business News Week (20 November 2026, T1–T2); Reuters / MillingandMillers on basmati contracts (T2).

3. Floriculture Exports

Floriculture is a high-value, low-volume category under APEDA (cut flowers, foliage, dried flowers, bulbs, rooted cuttings). It is not held in the FCI central pool.

Rs equivalent for 2025-26: approximately ₹818 crore. Fresh and dried cut flowers dominate the export basket. More than 300 export-oriented units.

Top destinations (2025-26 share of floriculture exports):

  • United States: 23.04%
  • Netherlands: 17.08% (major re-export hub into Europe)
  • United Arab Emirates: 9.37%
  • United Kingdom: 5.77%
  • Italy: 3.80%
  • Also listed among major markets: Canada, Malaysia.

Absolute values to France, Italy, Canada and the United Kingdom are modest relative to the United States and Netherlands. Area under floriculture (2024-25): ~397 thousand hectares. Production (2024-25): 3,226.20 thousand tonnes loose flowers + 1,039.29 thousand tonnes cut flowers.

Sources: APEDA Floriculture pages and AgriExchange top-destination tables (2025-26, T1); DGCIS-backed APEDA three-year statements (T1).

4. Relevance to Pehowa / Kurukshetra / Jhajjar MSME Operations

  • Haryana is a major contributor to national wheat and basmati rice production and procurement.
  • Surplus central-pool stocks support offtake programmes and can influence mandi price stability, though domestic wheat prices have remained firm in some centres.
  • Growing basmati demand in North America and Europe is relevant to regional exporters and processors.
  • Floriculture clusters are concentrated in southern and western states; limited direct production linkage to the Pehowa–Kurukshetra belt, but export data illustrates value-added agri diversification pathways.

5. Gaps and Uncertainties

  • Exact daily central-pool stock print as of 20 November 2026 is not published.
  • Destination-specific volume and value tables for France, Italy, Canada and the United Kingdom on non-basmati rice, maize or bajra are not available in the latest summary releases.
  • Precise 2025-26 country-by-country dollar values for floriculture beyond percentage shares are not fully published in the latest APEDA summaries.
  • Coarse-grain export volumes to the listed Western markets remain small relative to basmati and processed items.
